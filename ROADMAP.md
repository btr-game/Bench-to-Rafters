## The indie lane (owner, round 36)

Owner: "i think the game is way past the minigame inspired from the football game. so, i want to
switch lanes to an indie game." The game stays **free** (a donation channel, in a later round).
The order the owner set:

- **Round 36** (done): no cuts (owner: "i dont really want to cut anything"), three merges (Around
  you, Your life, the Body panel), and **the rafters finale** in place of Thank you for playing.
- **Endings for the other paths** (done in round 36): twelve endings, each with its scene and
  theme. Still open: a father whose son's career is played next reaches his ending only through the
  son's (the son's credits name him).
- **Round 37** (done): **a world to walk around**: the town in a career (the tabs as places on his
  street, every arena its own) and the main land (the start page as a map, reached by plane after
  the tutorial).
- **Round 38** (next; the plan is the Word document "Round 38 plan: story mode"): **story mode**, a
  cast of six (the friend, the rival, the mentor, the agent, the reporter, family), six chapters each
  at fixed moments of a career, with memories later chapters quote, met on the town's street, paid
  off in the finale. The owner's decisions: **its own mode** (a Story mode venue on the main land,
  one written hero, apart from the other careers); **mostly story, with a few real hooks** (the
  agent in contract talks, the mentor's plan edge, the friend as a trainer, the rival trade);
  **heavy at times, always with a gentle path**; **the player names his friend, his family and his
  rival** (the rest generated; skipping the naming generates all). Not asked, so the plan's
  defaults: the cast of six, and the reporter at a newsstand on the street.
  **The friend and the rival carry it** (owner: "the story mode will totally rely on the
  relationship between the three"): the friend is with him from the driveway up to the NBA, and
  how good he gets depends on the player's choices (out of the league early and his biggest fan,
  or a turn to coaching and later his assistant, or a huge leap past him to an MVP and a friendly
  rivalry to overcome); the rival is met in college and grows with him, always one small step
  ahead (an OVR just above his). Storylines: the friend joins his team, the rival joins his team,
  the rival and the friend on the same team, and a dramatic road where the rival badly injures the
  friend and the rivalry turns personal.
- **Round 39**: **The Floor**, one possession engine for every playable moment (crunch time, the
  playable playoff games, the finals action cam, 3-on-3). Owner: "this should be a high level game,
  with great detail and extensive design. we're gonna spend a lot of time building the interactions
  and animations." Discussed with the owner while 38 and 40 are built; built last, before publishing.
- **Round 40**: **languages**: Korean, Chinese, Japanese, Spanish and French. The last round with a
  significant system; after it, only small balance changes and bug fixes (owner: "so let's keep up
  the spark").
- **Publishing**: rounds 38, 39 and 40 go out together (owner: "when doing round 38, dont do the
  publishing"); priority is 38 and 40, then 39.
- **Later**: a donation channel.
- Turned down: careers framed as roguelite runs with meta-unlocks ("no").
- Still to weigh before any paid release: a fictional league in place of the NBA's teams, colours
  and legends.

## Round 33: three levels before the full game (built in round 33; DECISIONS.md has what was built)

Owner, after round 32: "from now on, the users have to play three levels to access the full game
(main homepage)". Beginner, then intermediate, then advanced; the full start page (every
difficulty, the challenges, the mini-games, the career log, the book, the community) opens only
after the third.

**Everyone** (owner: "every device from now on have to do it, since there were users who already
accessed the game but didnt properly play it"): devices that played before go through the levels
too; their saves, log and achievements stay on the device and are there when the full game opens.

**The way out** (owner, revised during round 33: "anyone who goes into the browser automatically
gets assigned to the tutorial, regardless of their data. but, at the end of the tutorial, the game
reveals the exit password ... on one corner or the bottom of the tutorial, there's a skip button,
where the user can insert the code to skip it. this allows old users to skip"):
- Every device starts in the tutorial, whatever it has saved.
- A Skip button sits in a corner of every tutorial page; it asks for the exit code, and the right
  code opens the full game at once.
- The graduation at the end of the tutorial shows the exit code, so a player who finished can skip
  on another device, or after the device forgets.
- Finishing the tutorial or entering the code opens the full game on that device for good.
- The check uses a hash of the code. Because the game must show the code at the end, the code
  itself is in the game too, lightly encoded so it does not appear as plain text in the page.
- The Developer door and the feedback channel stay on every page, in the levels too.

**The levels count nowhere in the community**: no boards, no feed, no live count of careers ended,
no statue or hall.

**Beginner** (decided):
- After the intro, the beginner career starts: no start page, no difficulty choice.
- The player picks a name, number, position and appearance; the rest is set (an archetype for the
  position, two focus ratings, USA).
- A difficulty a little easier than Regular guy, not as easy as Shoot for the stars; potential
  high enough that every beginner reaches the NBA.
- One high school season, one college season, draft night, then several NBA seasons (as many as
  fit in 5 to 10 minutes), then a graduation page.
- One plan a season (not a half), from six: Balanced, Grind, Shooting lab, Weight room, Film and
  reads, Lockdown.
- One hand-picked turning point a season, each teaching one thing (the risk tag, morale, coach
  trust, fame).
- The shop, cut down: three items a summer, one purchase.
- One playable last shot in his first NBA playoffs (the timing meter); the rest is simulated.
- Left out: staff, brands, press, nickname, rival, duos, team style, trades, the locker room, the
  coach's role talk, the farewell tour, the record watch, Love, mini-games, the Extras tab,
  community, Fast break, Sim and Master mode. Tabs: Home and Player. Settings: theme, sound, music,
  text size.
- No quitting: no New career, no Back to start, no Retire before the graduation page; a reload
  goes back into it.
- The graduation page leads to the levels page (intermediate and advanced), not the full start
  page.

**Revised after round 32** (owner):
- Intermediate is simpler than first proposed, and advanced only a step beyond it ("slightly more
  complicated than intermediate"). After the levels, the guide leads the player to a Regular guy
  career: highly suggested, not required.
- **The goal of every level is an NBA All-Star team.** The levels' difficulty is set so that at
  least 80% of careers make one (measured with the harness). A career that misses ends on a card of
  its own with sad music, and still the thank-you page.
- **Mini-games are not in the levels**, but passing the tutorial needs every mini-game played at
  least once with a C or better. Lockdown and Front Office already grade A to D; the others get a
  scale of their own.
- **The tutorial guide**: separate from Guide mode, which stays as it is for players who forget
  something. It covers everything the manual does except the achievements, the career log and the
  community, so that by the end the player knows every function as if they had read the project
  summary. Its steps cannot be skipped: while it asks for something, nothing else on the page
  responds.
- **What each level holds** (owner's final call):

  | | Beginner (5–10 min) | Intermediate (~10 min) | Advanced (~15 min) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Creation | name, number, position, look | + archetype, focus | + country (its road), hand |
  | Path | 1 high school, 1 college, draft, several NBA seasons | + a college from offers | + G League and overseas offers |
  | Plans | 1 a season, from 6 | 1 or 2 a season, all 12 | 1 or 2 a half (the full system) |
  | Turning points | 1 hand-picked a season | 1 a season, from the full pool | the full pool, and storylines |
  | Shop | 3 items, 1 purchase | the normal shop | + staff, wardrobe, collection |
  | Contracts | set for him | contract talks on the first NBA deal | every deal, and trade requests |
  | Season | results | standings, leaders, the bracket | + the coach card (role talk, hot seat), team style |
  | Playoffs | one last shot | playable games | + crunch time and the action cam |
  | NBA life | none | nickname, brands, rival (few choices each) | + starting five, body and weight |
  | Modes | none | Fast break | + Sim |
  | Goal | an All-Star team | an All-Star team | an All-Star team |

- **The closing tour**, after advanced, on a prepared star career, each step asking the player to
  do the thing: the locker room, Around the league with city and fans, duos and the Big Three, press
  conferences, the record watch, the farewell tour, the ring-chaser role, Love and the next
  generation, the Records and Extras tabs, undo, Simple text, Master mode and Settings. It ends on
  the full start page with "Start a Regular guy career (recommended)" lit up.
- **Missing the All-Star goal still passes the level** (the sad ending card, then on to the next).
- **Mini-games**: played from the levels page at any time after beginner, in any order; Front
  Office's C is making the playoffs once; the C marks for the three-point contest, the dunk contest,
  3-on-3, Legends and the lab come from test runs, for the owner to approve.
- **No stuck steps**: no emergency skip; instead every tutorial step is checked by a test that plays
  all three levels and the closing tour start to finish, on a computer and a phone, before release.
- **The levels page is a road map** (owner: "i like the word road map a lot ... make a map"): a
  pixel map with the high school (beginner), a college campus (intermediate) and an NBA arena
  (advanced) on the land, joined by a road, and the mini-games as an overseas league on an island
  across the water. His avatar drives a car between the places and takes a boat to the island. A
  checklist in the top-right corner, like a bucket list, ticks each goal as it is done (the three
  levels, then each mini-game at C or better).
- **No achievements** in the levels; **advanced counts nowhere** in the community either; a saved
  career from before waits until the full game opens; the full start page keeps a **Levels** card to
  replay any level.

## Kept for later (round 33)

## Round 35: multiplayer (built; see DECISIONS.md)

Owner: "let's start round 35. the theme here is multiplayer". Owner's picks: **Draft class with
friends** and **Challenge a friend**; players find each other **both** ways (an invite code or
link, and quick match). Defaults proposed, for the owner to confirm or change:

- **Challenge a friend** (asynchronous): from the end of a career, or any finished career in the
  career log, Challenge a friend makes a short code and link for the same starting player (seed,
  difficulty, position, archetype, focus, country, hand and look). The friend plays that prospect
  their own way; when their career ends the two sit side by side (the career log's Compare), the
  winner by career index, and the result reaches the challenger too. Quick match: an open-challenges
  board, to take on a stranger's prospect. Undo off, as for the daily challenge, so it stays fair.
- **Draft class with friends** (asynchronous): create a class (up to 8), share its code or link;
  quick match joins an open class with room. Every member starts on the class's seed, so the same
  league world and the same draft year, each with their own player. Each finished season posts to
  the class: a Class page with everyone's careers season by season, class standings by career
  index, and class awards each season (the class MVP and more). No one has to be online together.
  Later step: friends' players appear on their NBA teams in each other's leagues for the seasons
  they have already played.
- **Where**: the website and itch.io (the online database: new tables and functions, a migration),
  and the claude.ai page (its shared database). A copy with neither says multiplayer needs the
  online game.

## Kept from round 34

  number in sight while the card is folded.
- **Lessons and folded cards**: a lesson or a Guide mode step whose control sits in a folded card
